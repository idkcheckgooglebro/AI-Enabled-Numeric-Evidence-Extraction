Benthic Invertebrates as Indicators of Organic Pollution in the Western Baltic Sea

Abstract

The quantitative distribution of some important benthic invertebrates in a shallow inshore area of Kiel Bay (Western Baltic Sea) is described. This region is partly polluted by.domestic sewage from the municipality of Kiel. Three groups of species are distinguished with reference to their densities and other population parameters in the different subareas: Progressive species of the 1st and 2nd order indicate a high and moderate to slight degree of pollution respectively by high population numbers. Regressive species are adverse indicators, absent or occurring in exceptionally low numbers in affected areas. In many cases the examination must be concentrated on sand bottom, because a series of species normally dwelling on aufwuchs build up dense populations in sand, if it is organically enriched.

The polychaetes Capitella capitata, Nereis diversicolor and Polydora ligni are regarded as progressive species of the 1st order. The amphipods Corophium insidiosum and Gammarus salinus, the mussel Mytilus edulis, the polychaete Pygospio elegans, the snail Hydrobia ulvae, and some other invertebrates belong to the group of indicators for slight organic pollution. The main regressive species are the amphipod Bathyporeia sarsi and the mite Copidognathus fabriciusi.

1. Introduction

The indicator concept is based upon the assumption that relatively longlived, stationary organisms reflect the environmental conditions over a long time scale. In particular, a series of aquatic animals is regarded as closely associated with organic pollution and thus as significantly indicating this kind of man-made environmental change. A recent review of the multitude of already existing literature on this subject is given in the comprehensive report of LEppÄκoskI (1975).

In the present study, selected benthic invertebrates of the Western Baltic Sea were investigated for their possible value as indicator species. Their horizontal distribution in a gradient of domestic sewage pollution is described quantitatively in terms of abundance (= numerical density), biomass (dry weight), presence (=percentage of frequency), and dominance (= percentage of total population density).

The investigation area is a shallow inshore region in the South of Kiel Bay, where high quantities of domestic sewage are discharged. Following a transect from the sewage outlet along the coast line, three zones with a decreasing degree of pollution and with characteristic faunal assemblages were found on the sandy bottom at a depth of 3 m. More detailed descriptions and references to literature on the study area are given in previous papers (ANGER, 197õa, b). The dominant group within the macrofauna with respect to both number of individuals and of species was formed by polychaetes, followed by molluscs and amphipods. The only species which are treated in this report are those which show rather distinct reactions to the environmental gradient and fulfill the main requirenents for indicators recently summarized by ReisH (1972, 1973) and LEPPÄKOSKI (1975).

The investigation area was divided into three subregions (50-200, 300-700 and 1000—2000 m distance from the outfall). Within each subregion three stations were sampled and the data from these three stations were then combined to give one value for each subregion.

These subareas are nearly identical with the range of the associations defined by ANGER (1975b). They correspond to LEpPÄkoskI's "polluted", "semipolluted" and "healthy" bottoms. A "very polluted" zone sensu LEppÄkoskı is restricted to an area of less than 50 m radius around the outlet; a "semihealthy" botton has not been delimited in this survey. The original data for all stations are given in an unpublished thesis (University Kiel, 1975).

2. Methods

Nine stations were chosen in the investigation area. Two parallel samples per station per month were taken from 1971 to 1972 by means of a 0.1 m² van Veen grab, strained through a sieve with 1 mm mesh size and fixed in 40,'0 formalin solution. The material was sorted under a stereo microot            at  ts Additionally, one unstrained parallel sample was taken at each station with a smaller grab (0.01 m²) in order to gain information on meiofauna and young bottom stages. For further information on aims and methods and on some chemical characteristies of the sewage see ANGER (1975 b).

3.3. Regressive species

LEppÄkoskı's subgrouping into regressive species of 1st and 2nd orders could not be followed in this study, because it proved too restrictive to be applied to the material treated here. Since no species were found to be totally absent from all polluted parts of the investigation area, the following species correspond rather to regressive species of the 2nd order: Bathyporeia sarsi (amphipod), Pholoë minuta, Nephtys caeca, Protodrilus chaetifer (polychaetes), Copidognathus fabriciusi (mite),"Mysella bidentata (bivalve). Some of these species belong to the meiofauna, most of them occur rather scarcely even in undisturbed environnents. 4. Conclusions and Discussion

The species treated in this report can indicate different degrees of organic pollution in the reach of the Western Baltic, in a few cases also for further areas. They can be divided into three groups according to their response to sedimenting sewage particles and the subsequent environmental changes (Tab. 2). Most of them should be studied on aufwuchs-free sand, as they have clearer reactions on this substrate if affected by organic enrichment.

These results are in fairly good accordance with those of a preliminary pilot study (AnGER, 1975a). In some cases there are differences which can be explained partly by the simple methods in combination with the purely qualitative statistics used for the pilot study. Nevertheless it could be shown that benthic organisms are able to serve as indicators of environmental quality. Many other authors (see LEppÄkoskI, 1975)

5. Summary

Three groups of indicator species are distinguished on the basis of their distribution pattern in a pollution gradient observed in the south of Kiel Bay (Western Baltic Sea). Three polychaetous annelids show distinct indicator qualities by inhabiting polluted sand bottoms in high nunibers. Some other bentic invertebrates occur mainly under conditions of slight organic pollution; a few species can be regarded as very sensitive, thus indicating pollution by their absence or exceptionally low numbers.
